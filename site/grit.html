Grit Lab

36 paddle faces under a 4K microscope.

We photograph every paddle's surface up close, match it to the brand's own name for its grit, and track how it wears after real play.

How we test

W

Three lenses

Elikliv 4K digital microscope. W: whole area. L: about 10 to 20 weave squares across. M: about 5 to 7 squares across, the standard lens for the collection.

ID

Surface ID

Every surface is photographed, then matched to the brand's own published name for its grit, so labels reflect what the brand calls it.

%

Wear against the throat

The throat barely touches the ball, so it shows what the grit looked like new. We compare hitting zones to it at the same zoom and light, and judge only sharp frames from a full-face video scan.

What we've noticed so far: larger, coarser grit tends to feel sticky, while finer grit tends to feel softer and plusher. Big raised crystals take every hit directly, so they dull and chip fastest. Fine grit set inside the coating wears by slow polishing instead.

Newest additions

Arronax Boost-1

Green hexagonal flakes set into a frosted T700 carbon weave, with fine raised grit points between them. Arronax calls it a GRITBITE surface on the BOOST-1 QuickSand but doesn't say what GritBite is made of. The hex flakes look decorative; the weave texture and raised grit appear to do the gripping. That's a visual read and hasn't been spin-tested.

Volair CMD

Volair licensed 11SIX24's HexGrit. Under matched light, a brand-new CMD shows the same kind of glassy, beaded layer with fine sparkle as the 11SIX24 HexGrit. The weave looked slightly crisper under the Volair's layer, which could be focus or the spot photographed.
