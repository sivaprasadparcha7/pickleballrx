How the test works

PaddleClamped in a vise, tilted back about 30 to 35° from vertical
Ball feedRallie ball machine, about 34 to 39 mph at the paddle
CameraGoPro, 1080p at 960 frames per second
BallMarked with thick black lines so its rotation can be read
SpinWorked out from how far the markings turn between frames
Speed adjustmentEvery result is scaled to a 37.5 mph incoming ball (spin × 37.5 ÷ actual incoming speed)
Target impact angleBall meets the face at about 34°
Hits countedFace and throat hits; balls that hit the vise or miss are excluded

The three numbers we report

  • Spin (average): the average of the paddle's best hits (best 3 or best 5; the two differ by about 20 RPM).
  • Top spin (highest hit): the single highest hit.
  • Rebound speed: average ball speed coming off the paddle, a simple indicator of power.

Limits

  • This is a static rig with no swing, so on-court spin will be higher and varies by player. It's a tool for comparing paddles with each other and with themselves over time.
  • It is not an official USA Pickleball test. Disclaimers.
  • One sample of each paddle was tested. Paddles were tested on different days and have different use histories.
  • Where the ball lands on the face changes the result: the lower-middle of the face spins the most, the tip the least.
  • Results are adjusted for ball speed but not for small differences in impact angle between sessions. The rig was re-set during October 2026, and numbers from different setups aren't directly comparable.
